# Milestone 25: Voice

Status: spec 09-10-2026, agreed in chat with Mark (push-to-talk by default, anyone nearby
hears you, our own codec in GDScript).

Talk to the pirates around you. Your voice comes out of your pirate, so it fades with
distance and crews out of earshot hear nothing. Enemy crews hear you too, as in Sea of
Thieves.

## What you get

- **Hold V to talk.** A mic icon shows in the corner while you are being sent.
- **Open mic** instead, in the Esc menu: you are sent whenever the mic is louder than a level
  you set, and for 0.4 s after (so the ends of words aren't cut off).
- **Heard from the speaker.** Full volume within 5 m, fading, gone at 40 m. That covers a
  whole ship and shouting across to a ship alongside. Same for every crew.
- **Who is talking.** A speaker icon over a pirate's head while you hear them.
- **Esc menu, "Voice"**: voice on or off; hold V or open mic; voice volume; which microphone;
  a level bar to check your mic before you sail (with the open-mic level marked on it); a mute
  box for each other player (for this session).
- **Controls card (H)** gains "Hold V: talk to anyone nearby".

## How it works

1. **Mic.** The microphone plays into a muted bus ("Mic") with a capture effect on it, so the
   game can read it but you never hear yourself. Read every frame, mixed to mono and brought
   down to 16,000 samples a second (each output sample the average of the input it covers,
   which also stops high hiss folding down into the voice).
2. **Gate.** Push-to-talk or open mic decides whether those samples are sent.
3. **Packing.** Every 60 ms (960 samples) becomes one packet: a sequence number, then the
   samples squeezed 4:1 with IMA ADPCM, a standard and simple codec. Each packet carries its
   own codec state, so a lost packet costs only its own 60 ms. 485 bytes, about 65 kbit/s
   while talking and nothing while quiet.
4. **To the server** on a channel of its own, unreliable (a late voice packet is useless, and
   voice must never hold up the game's own updates).
5. **Server.** Forwards each packet to every player within 45 m of the speaker (a little past
   the 40 m where they would stop hearing it, so voices fade out rather than cut off). It
   refuses packets over 600 bytes and more than 25 a second from one player (a little over the
   17 a second a talker sends), so nobody can flood the others.
6. **Playing it.** Each speaker gets a 3D sound source on their pirate's head, on a "Voice"
   bus with its own volume. Packets wait in a short queue: play starts once two have arrived
   (120 ms), an old or repeated packet is dropped, and if the queue grows past six (360 ms
   behind) the oldest go, so a voice never drifts further and further behind.

A game hosted from the title screen (server and player in one window) works the same way:
the host's voice goes straight to the server part, and voices for the host are played
directly.

The dedicated server and the test bots have no sound at all. Bots can still receive voice
(they count what they hear), and a test-only option `--voice-tone` makes a bot "speak": it
sends a tone through the real packing and network path, two seconds on and one off, as if
holding V.

## Settings saved

In `user://settings.cfg`, section `voice`: `on` (true), `open_mic` (false), `volume` (0.8),
`level` (open-mic level, 0.1), `device` ("Default"). Mutes are not saved.

## Files

| File | What it does |
|---|---|
| `game/voice/adpcm.gd` | IMA ADPCM encode/decode |
| `game/voice/voice_packet.gd` | One packet: sequence number + ADPCM state + samples |
| `game/voice/downsampler.gd` | Any input rate to 16 kHz mono, by averaging |
| `game/voice/talk_gate.gd` | Push-to-talk or open mic (with the 0.4 s hang-on) |
| `game/voice/voice_queue.gd` | One speaker's queue: order, start delay, catch-up |
| `game/voice/mic.gd` | The microphone (or the test tone): 16 kHz samples and a level |
| `game/voice/voice.gd` | Ties it together on each player's machine; plays each speaker |
| `game/session/server_sim.gd` | `voice_listeners(id)` and the per-player cap |
| `game/net/net.gd` | Two messages: voice to the server, voice from a speaker |
| `game/ui/hud.gd`, `game/player/avatar.gd` | Mic icon, speaker icon, Esc menu, controls card |
| `project.godot`, `export_presets.cfg` | Mic input on; Mac microphone permission and message |

## Tests

- Unit (`tests/test_voice.gd`): codec round trip clean enough (signal-to-noise over 20 dB on a
  voice-like test signal); packets come back whole and a broken one is refused; 48 kHz and
  44.1 kHz both come down to 16 kHz at the right pitch; the gate for both modes, including the
  hang-on; the queue's start delay, order, repeats and catch-up; who is in earshot (near yes,
  far no, other crews yes, never yourself); the server's cap.
- Smoke (`tests/smoke/voice_test.sh`): one server, a bot speaking on crew 1's ship, a
  crew-mate on the same ship and a bot on crew 2's ship far away. The crew-mate must hear the
  speaker, the far bot must hear nothing.

## Not tested here

A real microphone, the Mac permission prompt, Windows' microphone privacy setting and how it
actually sounds. `docs/play-test-checklist.md` gets a voice section for those.
