# Milestone 26: Shipyards, and the first playtest's fixes

Status: spec 12-10-2026, for Mark's review. Built from the first playtest's notes and Mark's
answers in chat. All four changes ship together as 0.5.0.

1. A sunk ship no longer comes back by itself. You buy a new one at a shipyard.
2. Buckets take out more water.
3. The game stops hiding the mouse when you leave the window.
4. The way to the first mission is easy to see.

## 1. Ships are bought

**Starting.** Everyone starts on Port Merrow's pier, a short walk from Moll's lighthouse.
Each crew's first ship (the galleon) is moored at Merrow for free: crews 1 and 2 alongside
the pier, where you step from the pier onto her ladder; crews 3 and 4 a few strokes further
out. Anyone who joins a crew that has no ship also starts on the pier.

**Sinking.** When she goes down, she stays gone. The crew is left in the water where she
sank (as now) and swims ashore. Any chests or crates aboard float (as now).

**Dying.** If your crew has a ship, you come back aboard (as now). If not, you come back on
Port Merrow's beach.

**Shipyards.** There are four: Port Merrow (Old Hobb on his pier, who also does refits),
Cutlass Cay, Parrot Rock and Gull Isle. Each is a small jetty with a shipwright and a
signboard. There are none on the fort, Isla Calavera (the Spanish) or the story's rocks.
Press E at a shipwright for a card with two ships:

| Ship | Price | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| The galleon | 500 gold, from the buyer's own gold | The ship you have now: two masts, eight guns, a hold, the map table |
| The sloop | Free | A tiny boat for about three: one mast, a wheel, an anchor and a small map table. No guns and no hold. Quicker and nimbler than the galleon, but fragile: water rises faster through each hole and less of it sinks her |

- The new ship appears at that yard's berth with the buyer aboard. Crew-mates swim or walk
  out to her.
- **One ship per crew.** A crew with a sloop can buy the galleon (trading up), and the sloop
  is broken up. A crew that still has a galleon can't buy another one.
- A new galleon wears the crew's refits (sails, hull, flag, figurehead, trophies). The sloop
  has one plain look.
- **Missions work from the sloop** (her map table has the same board), so a broke crew can earn
  the 500. Story steps that need the ship's cannons wait until the crew has a galleon again.
- Skeleton ships and other crews can attack a sloop like any ship. She can only run.

## 2. Buckets

A bucket now takes out 0.2 m of water instead of 0.06 m. One hole lets in 0.012 m a second,
and a trip with the bucket (scoop below, climb, throw) takes about 8 s. Before, one pirate
bailing could not keep up with even one hole. Now one keeps ahead of two. Patching is still
what saves a badly holed ship. The sinking line stays at 2.4 m of water.

## 3. The mouse

In the game, the mouse is held by the window so you can look around. When the window lost
focus (alt-tab, or a click outside), coming back grabbed the mouse straight away. So you
could never reach the title bar to move or maximise the window, or click its buttons.

- When the window loses focus, the game lets go of the mouse and keeps it free. It grabs it
  again only when you click inside the game.
- While the mouse is free and no menu is open, "Click to play" shows in the middle of the
  screen.
- **F11 toggles full screen**, and there is a "Full screen" switch in the Esc menu. It is
  remembered.

## 4. The way to the first mission

- You start on Merrow's pier, near Moll.
- While your crew has no chapter under way, the compass shows a **gold marker** towards whoever
  gives the next one (Moll for the first). A line under the compass says **"Next: speak to
  Moll Tinder at the lighthouse"**. The marker and the line go once the chapter starts, and the
  mission card takes over, as now.
- If the next act needs a higher pirate level, the line says so and there is no marker.

## How it fits together

- **Two hull shapes.** Today one fixed layout describes the ship (deck heights, ladders,
  stations, holes, the hold), and about 300 places in the code read it. It becomes one layout
  per hull, galleon and sloop, and each of those places asks the ship it is dealing with for
  its own layout. The galleon's layout is unchanged, so the galleon plays exactly as before.
- **The sloop:** about 11 m long and 4 m wide, deck 1.3 m above the water. Stations are the
  wheel, the sail ropes, the anchor and the map table. The holes are in the open hull below
  the deck boards: patch them from the deck. Scoop water anywhere on deck while there is
  some, and throw it over the rail. She sinks at 0.9 m of water. Top speed is 15 % above the
  galleon, and she turns 40 % quicker. A new Blender model (`art/blender/sloop.py`), in the
  same toon style.
- **Shipyards:** a jetty, a shipwright and a sign at a fixed spot on each island's shore,
  with a berth beside the jetty. Three new shipwrights stand at the island yards (new
  pirate looks). Old Hobb runs Merrow's.
- **The server decides** every purchase (enough gold, one ship per crew, the buyer standing at
  the yard) and tells the crew.

## Tests

- **Unit:**

  - no new ship after sinking
  - dying with and without a ship
  - buying: gold taken, refused when short, the sloop free, trading up, refits kept, one ship

  - a broke bot swims to Gull Isle's yard, takes the free sloop and sails her
  - a bot with 600 gold buys a galleon at Merrow
